# Literature audit

Checked on 26 November 2026. The goal was to verify the exact question, identify the closest prior results, and check for an antecedent to the labeled finite-graph counterexample.

- **Exact source.** Edward John Mottram, *The geometry of non-Markovian interacting systems*, University of Cambridge, 2015, §3.1.4 and Example 3.1.5, printed pp. 43–44. The thesis explicitly asks whether the final law is positively associated. Its path on four vertices violates the FKG lattice condition for every positive rate. Mottram states that the same path has positive association. Definition 3.2.1 gives the finite homogeneous model used here. [Cambridge dissertation](https://doi.org/10.17863/CAM.15980)
- **Earlier primary paper by Mottram.** *Percolation with constant freezing*, Journal of Statistical Physics **155** (2014), 932–965. The preprint’s Definition 2.1 specifies finite constant-freezing dynamics. Section 1.4 discusses related correlation and monotonicity questions. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-014-0985-z) · [Full preprint, version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1752v2)
- **Mean-field antecedent.** Eli Ben-Naim and Pavel L. Krapivsky, *Percolation with multiple giant clusters*, Journal of Physics A **38** (2005), L417–L423. Mottram credits this work with introducing the mean-field case. It analyzes cluster sizes and successive gelation transitions using the edge-rate scaling on the complete graph. The checked paper does not state the labeled event-pair covariance or the finite association counterexample. [Original preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0501218)
- **Other freezing mechanisms.** David J. Aldous’s tree model freezes infinite clusters. Jacob van den Berg, Demeter Kiss and Pierre Nolin analyze volume-threshold freezing on the triangular lattice. Those freezing rules differ from the constant-rate finite warm-component rule in the question. [Aldous](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004199004326) · [Van den Berg–Kiss–Nolin](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2371)
- **Catalog record.** The pinned Q277 entry records positive association for graphs whose connected components have at most four edges. It asks about the general finite-graph law. The complete graph on four vertices has six edges. [Pinned Q277](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/probability-stochastic-processes-part-1.md#q277)

The metadata and searchable excerpts were checked. Full-text review was unavailable. [ETH thesis record](https://doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000329137) · [ETH departmental notice](https://math.ethz.ch/news-and-events/news/d-math-news/2018/12/doctoral-exam-of-mayra-bermudez-contreras.html)
